Privacy policy

Last updated 8 October 2026

What Spog collects

Spog collects the photos you take in the app, the country you spot in, and the city name if you turn location on. Each identification also carries two anonymous identifiers: the device identifier provided by Apple, and an installation identifier drawn at random by the app and stored in your iPhone's keychain, which survives a reinstall. They are used only to count your scans and renders for the day, to cap how many requests can be made and to prevent abuse. They are tied to no identity, and our server keeps only a fingerprint of them, along with what you used that day. Nothing else: no advertising identifier, no behavioral tracking, no third-party analytics.

Your photos

Photos are stored on your device. To identify a vehicle, a photo is sent to our server, which forwards it to an artificial-intelligence provider for analysis, then returns the result. The code of the country where the photo was taken goes with it, to estimate the car's value on that country's market and in its currency; it reveals nothing beyond that country. When you switch a card to studio, its photo is sent the same way to generate its studio render, which is then stored on your device. Before any transmission, license plates detected in the image are blurred on your device. By default, transmitted photos are not kept once the answer is returned. If you agree — you are asked once, and the "Help recognize cars" setting lets you change your mind — the photos of your catches are kept on our server, with plates already blurred, along with the identified model and any correction you made, to train recognition that will run directly on the iPhone. They are tied to no identity, and are never published, sold or shared.

Country and location

Location is optional and the app works entirely without it. When it is on, your position is turned into a country code and a city name straight away, then dropped. No coordinates are kept or transmitted, and no movement history is built. Permission is requested for “while using the app” only. On the scan screen, your position is also used to measure your speed, so that scanning locks itself if you are driving. That measurement stays on the device, is neither stored nor transmitted, and stops as soon as you leave the screen.

No account

Spog asks for no email address and no password. For leaderboards, our server recognizes your player through the anonymous installation identifier, and keeps your catches: the model, the country, the date, the points, and what it takes to redraw each card (its number, its paint, its estimated value) — never your photos, unless you agreed to share them for training. The handle you choose is visible to the other players in your league. Sign in with Apple is optional: it is only used to get your cards back on another device (without their photos, which never leave yours), and Spog receives nothing from Apple but an anonymous identifier, neither your name nor your email. You can delete your account at any time in Settings.

Third parties

Only vehicle identification involves a third party, and only for the photo being analyzed and the code of the country where it was taken. Nothing is sold, rented or shared for advertising purposes. Apple handles subscription payments; we never see your payment details.

How long data is kept

Your cards and photos stay on your device for as long as you keep them. Removing a card from the garage deletes its photos immediately and permanently, and removes it from the leaderboards too. Catches recorded for the leaderboards are kept on our server for as long as your account exists; deleting it, in Settings, erases everything. Your referral code, any code you entered, and the vehicles the app has learned from your scans live on your device, and go away with the app.

Your rights

No data is linked to your real identity on our side. Deleting your account, in Settings, erases your handle, your recorded catches and your ranking. The training photos you agreed to share are all deleted as soon as you turn off the "Help recognize cars" setting. Everything else is in your hands, on your device. You may withdraw location permission at any time in the iOS Settings app.
